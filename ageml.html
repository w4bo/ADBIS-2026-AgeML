Data-Centric AI: Main Point

Data-Centric AI shifts attention from only tuning models to improving the data and preprocessing choices that shape model behavior (Zha et al. 2025).

For practitioners, that means:

  • Data properties should influence pipeline structure
  • Domain assumptions should be explicit, reusable, and inspectable
  • Automation should reduce routine work without hiding critical decisions

Keep this as the first conceptual anchor: the paper starts from the data-centric view. Human knowledge is valuable because preprocessing and validation decisions often determine whether an ML workflow is robust.

Background: AutoML and Agents

AutoML gives algorithmic search

  • Automates (only) model selection and hyperparameter optimization (He et al. 2021)
    • Classic systems include TPOT, Auto-WEKA, and Auto-Sklearn (Olson and Moore 2019; Thornton et al. 2013; Feurer et al. 2022)
  • Efficient and end-to-end variants include FLAML and HAMLET (Wang et al. 2022; Francia et al. 2023)

Agentic AI gives generation and adaptation

  • Uses LLM-backed agents for planning, code generation, validation, and repair
    • Recent examples include DS-Agent, MLZero, and AutoML-Agent (Guo et al. 2024; Fang et al. 2025; Trirat et al. 2025)
  • Needs specifications and guardrails to stay auditable

Data scientists augment knowledge to drive selection and optimization

  • End-to-end AutoML (Francia et al. 2023, 2024) and LLM-driven agents (Trirat et al. 2025), but controllability and traceability remain open problems.

AGE-ML in One Slide

Given external domain knowledge (if any), LLM-powered agents distill human-readable specifications describing assumptions and constraints

  • If no knowledge is provided, we start with classic ML specification
  • Specification is written in YAML

AGE-ML in One Slide

Data scientists (and experts) can read, revise, and manually augment the specification

  • The search space is not just optimized by algorithms, it is shaped by experts.

AGE-ML in One Slide

Planner and executor agents generate, validate, repair, and run pipelines under the constraints imposed by the specification.

  • Agents can synthesize and adapt code, but they work inside an explicit search space rather than freely inventing the workflow.
  • AGE-ML returns trained pipelines together with code, metrics, artifacts, and natural-language explanations for audit and reuse.
  • The output is not only a score or a model. It is a governed ML process with traceable evidence

Specification in, governed pipelines out.

Agentic architecture

Planner

Specification to admissible pipelines (admissibility as a finite-domain constraint satisfaction problem, Z3 to compute admissible pipelines).

Executor

From pipelines to code, runs, and explanations

Catalog

Collect scripts, metrics, artifacts, traces

Evaluator

Best model under the budget

AGE-ML is positioned as the approach that combines end-to-end automation, explicit constraints, pipeline validation, and MLOps-oriented traceability.

The point is not that every other system lacks value. The comparison highlights the particular combination AGE-ML targets: constrained generation with LLM-backed automation, interpretable pipelines, cold-start capabilities, and auditable execution traces.

Evaluation and Results

Evaluation setup

  • Datasets: 6 (Classification) + 3 (Regression)
  • Metrics: balanced accuracy for classification; RMSE for regression
  • Budget: 30 pipelines, 10 generation attempts per pipeline, 2 hours per run
  • Baselines: HAMLET for classification (closest approach for domain knowledge injection); Kaggle notebooks for regression

Results at a glance

  • Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite beats the baseline on 5 / 9 datasets
  • Gemini 2.5 Flash beats the baseline on 4 / 9 datasets

What We Learn

We need inspectable loop where human knowledge shapes what automation is allowed to do rather than a larger AutoML black box.

  1. Specification narrows automation without removing flexibility.
  2. Symbolic planning keeps the search space inspectable and coherent.
  3. The validation loop matters as much as the LLM.
  4. The catalog turns generated ML work into governed ML work.

Limitations and Next Steps

Current limitations

  • One coherent specification is assumed, but specification evolve over time and must be revised
  • LLM generation can fail under strict time and retry budgets
  • Results may vary across repeated runs

Future work

  • Support specification evolution and revision
  • Enable feedbacks on pipeline execution and optimization
  • Suggest specification extensions and refinements after pipeline execution

Specification as Control Surface

pipeline:
  steps:
    normalization:
      candidates:
        min-max:
          params: {feature_range: [[0, 1]]}
    classification:
      mandatory: true
      candidates:
        knn:
          params:
            n_neighbors: [5, 11]
            weights: [uniform, distance]
ordering:
  - sequence: [imputation, normalization, features]
  - {before: features, after: classification}
constraints:
  - if: {step: {classification: knn}}
    require: [normalization, features]
budgets:
  pipelines: 5
  generation_attempts: 10

Do not dwell on syntax. Emphasize readability: YAML-like, parsable, and suitable for both human editing and agent-assisted generation or revision.

Traceability Is a First-Class Result

Logging hierarchy

Every experiment records:

  • The specification and dataset context
  • Each admissible pipeline selected by the planner
  • Generated scripts, hyperparameters, metrics, artifacts, and explanations

This gives MLOps-style provenance for agent-generated ML work.

This is where the system becomes more than a benchmark runner. The audience should see that AGE-ML tries to make agentic automation auditable.

Execution: The Agentic Loop

Executor state machine

Generated code must pass two gates:

  • Semantic validation: implements exactly the intended pipeline
  • Runtime validation: trains and evaluates under the execution budget

Failures become feedback for bounded repair attempts.

The important point is that code generation is not single-shot. Generation, checking, execution, and explanation are one loop, bounded by the specification and the budget.
